Mission & Governance

About the Liberation War Archive

Safeguarding Bangladesh's national memory through scholarly digital preservation, rigorous provenance verification, and open historical access.

Institutional Purpose

Why Preserving Historical Records Matters

A nation that loses its records loses its conscience. The Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971 was one of the most heroic and tragic struggles for self-determination in modern human history. Yet decades after independence, primary paper documents degrade, photographic negatives fade, and the brave men and women who lived through 1971 are passing away.

The Liberation War Archive was conceived to address this existential archival imperative. Operating as an open digital repository, the archive collects, restores, digitizes, and cross-references original historical records from national archives, foreign diplomatic repositories, and private family collections.

By establishing clear provenance and providing open access without political bias, we empower educators, researchers, international jurists, and new generations of citizens to engage directly with the authentic truth of 1971.

Primary Documents

Original charters, declarations, telegrams, and military orders provide indisputable legal and historical proof of democratic legitimacy and military capitulation.

Oral Testimonies

Recorded voices preserve human emotion, tactical nuances, and the visceral reality of survival that official communiqués inevitably omit.

Photographic Negatives

Authentic visual records capture the human faces of guerrilla fighters, the devastation of the genocide, and the unrepeatable euphoria of liberation.

Historical Context

The Journey to Independence: 1948–1971

1948–1952

The Language Movement (ভাষার সংগ্রাম)

When the Pakistani state sought to impose Urdu as the sole state language, the people of Bengal rose in cultural defiance. On February 21, 1952, students gave their lives in Dhaka, sowing the fundamental seeds of Bengali national identity.

1966

The Historic Six-Point Movement (ছয় দফা কর্মসূচি)

Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman announced the legendary Six-Point Charter of Demands, demanding regional autonomy, separate currency accounts, and fiscal sovereignty to end economic exploitation of East Bengal.

1970

The 1970 General Elections (গণরায়)

In the first universal adult franchise election in Pakistan's history, the Awami League won 167 out of 169 seats in East Pakistan, securing an absolute democratic majority in the 313-seat National Assembly.

March 1971

Non-Cooperation & Operation Searchlight

Following the postponement of the assembly, civil disobedience took hold. On March 25, the Pakistan military unleashed Operation Searchlight. Bangabandhu declared independence before being taken prisoner.

April–Dec 1971

The 9-Month Armed War & Final Liberation

The Provisional Government of Bangladesh organized the war across 11 sectors. On December 16, 1971, Pakistani forces signed the Instrument of Surrender in Dhaka, marking the triumphant birth of sovereign Bangladesh.

Our Preservation Methodology & Standards

Provenance & Verification

Every archival item must possess verifiable chain-of-custody documentation. We correlate records with official government gazettes, wartime publications from Swadhin Bangla Betar Kendra, and international repositories.

Ethical Oral History Collection

Interviews are conducted with sensitive trauma-informed care and full informed consent. Participants or their surviving heirs retain the right to specify confidentiality embargoes or open educational release.

High-Resolution Digitization

Documents and photographs are scanned at true uncompressed 600–1200 DPI optical resolution. Color targets and archival gray cards are used during digitization to ensure color accuracy.

Non-Fabrication Guarantee

We never use AI-generated images or fabricated documents. All items displayed on this platform are authentic historical artifacts, and demonstration content is clearly labeled.
