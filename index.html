Historical Overview · 1971

The Significance of Preserving 1971 History

The 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War was not merely a military conflict; it was an epochal mass struggle for human dignity, democratic sovereignty, and cultural survival.

Spanning nine heroic and harrowing months—from the brutal crackdown of Operation Searchlight on March 25, 1971, to the historic unconditional surrender of Pakistani forces on December 16, 1971—the people of Bangladesh endured one of the twentieth century's most devastating genocides. Three million lives were sacrificed, more than two hundred thousand women suffered war trauma, and ten million refugees were forced across borders into open camps.

In the face of overwhelming military force, ordinary farmers, students, workers, and rebellious armed personnel forged the Mukti Bahini (Liberation Army). Supported by international solidarity and coordinated by the Provisional Government at Mujibnagar, they triumphed against tyrannical military subjugation.

The Liberation War Archive is established to safeguard these fragile physical documents, audio recordings, and photographic negatives from decay and distortion. By making these primary sources accessible to scholars, educators, and the global public, we ensure that the sacrifices of 1971 remain an eternal beacon of justice.

Chronicle of Freedom

Milestones of the 1971 Liberation War

A chronological roadmap marking the defining turning points in Bangladesh's struggle for independence.

March 7, 1971

The Historic 7th March Address

Bangabandhu delivers his immortal extempore call for independence to two million citizens at Ramna Race Course, outlining civil disobedience directives.

March 25–26, 1971

Operation Searchlight & Independence Declared

The Pakistan Army initiates a brutal military onslaught across Dhaka. Bangabandhu proclaims the Independence of Bangladesh before his arrest.

April 17, 1971

Swearing-in of the Mujibnagar Government

The Provisional Government of Bangladesh is formally sworn in at Baidyanathtala, Meherpur, establishing legitimate wartime governance and the Mukti Bahini command.

August 1, 1971

The Concert for Bangladesh (New York)

George Harrison and Pandit Ravi Shankar organize the historic benefit concert at Madison Square Garden, mobilizing global public conscience and UNICEF relief funds.

December 14, 1971

Martyred Intellectuals Tragedy

On the eve of military collapse, occupying forces and local collaborators systematically abduct and execute Bangladesh's finest scholars, doctors, and journalists.

December 16, 1971

The Instrument of Surrender & Victory

Pakistani armed forces unconditionally surrender to the joint command of India and Bangladesh at Ramna Race Course. Sovereign Bangladesh is born.

Preserving History with Uncompromising Integrity

Every document, testimony, and photograph within the Liberation War Archive is cataloged in accordance with international archival metadata standards. We work in close dialogue with academic historians, national museums, and surviving families to ensure absolute authenticity and historical rigor.

Archival Access Principles

  • Authenticity Verification: Primary sources are cross-referenced with national gazettes and international diplomatic records.
  • Open Educational Access: Free digital access for researchers, students, and citizens worldwide.
  • Ethical Oral Preservation: Respectful handling of traumatic testimonies under informed archival consent.
